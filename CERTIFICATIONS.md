# Certification Roadmaps
> This curriculum is **not** a substitute for any licensing exam or its official prep — but it builds the underlying knowledge so that, when you choose a credential, you're studying the *exam format* rather than learning the material for the first time. Here's how the path maps to the major credentials.

## Intuit Academy Bookkeeping / QuickBooks Badge  
*Entry-level, vendor-backed bookkeeping credential. Intuit Academy offers a genuinely free self-paced bookkeeping program (16 modules) culminating in a badge exam; the same content is mirrored as the 'Intuit Academy Bookkeeping Professional Certificate' on Coursera, which is free to audit (the shareable certificate requires the ~$39/month subscription). Covers the accounting cycle, recording transactions, adjusting entries, financial statements, and basic analysis. There is no formal QuickBooks 'certification' here per se, but the badge signals employability for junior bookkeeping roles and pairs naturally with QuickBooks software practice.*
**Region:** Global (US-centric content; badge issued in the U.S.) · **Typical cost:** Free (Intuit Academy badge) to ~$39/month if you want the verified Coursera certificate; auditing the Coursera course is free.

**Who it's for:** Career-changers and absolute beginners who want a fast, free, recognizable first credential before committing to a heavier cert (NACPB/AIPB) or a degree.

**Prerequisites:** None. No degree or experience required.

**Maps to modules:** Financial Accounting Fundamentals, The Accounting Cycle & Adjusting Entries, Financial Statements, Bookkeeping Software (QuickBooks)

## NACPB Certified Public Bookkeeper (CPB)  
*US professional bookkeeping license from the National Association of Certified Public Bookkeepers. Earned by passing three 50-question exams — Bookkeeping, QuickBooks, and Payroll (75% to pass each) — plus an experience requirement. A strong, recognized US bookkeeping credential that signals readiness to run a small-business books end-to-end. Distinct from (and lighter than) the CPA.*
**Region:** United States · **Typical cost:** Exam fees ~$240 (members) to ~$300 (non-members) for all three parts; additional membership/course costs if not waived.

**Who it's for:** Bookkeepers seeking a formal US license and small-practice owners who want a credibility marker with clients.

**Prerequisites:** Complete NACPB courses in accounting, payroll, and QuickBooks (or waive via accredited transcripts) + ~1 year / 2,000 hours of supervised bookkeeping experience (or 3 months via the CPB program). 24 hours CPE/year to maintain.

**Maps to modules:** Financial Accounting Fundamentals, The Accounting Cycle & Adjusting Entries, Payroll Accounting, Bookkeeping Software (QuickBooks), Financial Statements

## AIPB Certified Bookkeeper (CB)  
*The American Institute of Professional Bookkeepers' CB designation — the other major US bookkeeping credential alongside NACPB's CPB. A four-part national exam covering adjusting entries, error correction, payroll, depreciation, inventory, internal controls and fraud prevention. Parts 1 and 2 are taken at a test center; Parts 3 and 4 are open-book take-home tests bundled with the workbooks. Well-regarded by employers.*
**Region:** United States · **Typical cost:** Self-study workbooks ~$254 (member) to ~$314 (list); plus registration and test-center fees. The official prep materials are not free (only the published syllabus is).

**Who it's for:** Working bookkeepers who want a rigorous, employer-recognized US credential and are willing to document real experience.

**Prerequisites:** Recommended: be a working bookkeeper or have ~1 year of college accounting. Must document 2 years full-time (or 3,000 hours part-time) bookkeeping experience within 3 years of passing. Adherence to a code of ethics + CPE to maintain.

**Maps to modules:** The Accounting Cycle & Adjusting Entries, Payroll Accounting, Inventory & Cost Flow, Depreciation & Fixed Assets, Internal Controls & Fraud Prevention

## ACCA Foundations in Accountancy (FIA) / Certified Accounting Technician (CAT)  
*ACCA's on-ramp suite for those without a degree. Builds from Recording Financial Transactions (FA1) and Management Information (MA1) up through Business & Technology (FBT), Financial Accounting (FFA) and Management Accounting (FMA), plus Foundations in Professionalism. Awards stackable diplomas and the CAT qualification, and provides exemptions into the full ACCA Qualification. On-demand computer-based exams. A globally portable entry path.*
**Region:** Global (UK-headquartered; strong in UK, EU, Africa, Middle East, South/SE Asia) · **Typical cost:** Per-exam CBE fees paid to test centers plus ACCA registration/annual subscription; varies by country. Generally low hundreds of GBP across the suite.

**Who it's for:** School-leavers and beginners without university access who want an internationally recognized, exemption-yielding route toward full ACCA.

**Prerequisites:** Open entry — no formal academic prerequisites for the Foundations level. Registration + annual subscription fee required.

**Maps to modules:** Financial Accounting Fundamentals, Managerial / Cost Accounting, Business & Technology / Economics, Financial Statements, Business Law & Ethics

## CPA — Certified Public Accountant (US)  
*The gold-standard US accounting license. Under the 2024+ CPA Evolution model the exam is three Core sections — Auditing (AUD), Financial Accounting & Reporting (FAR), Regulation/Tax (REG) — plus one Discipline of your choice: Business Analysis & Reporting (BAR), Information Systems & Controls (ISC), or Tax Compliance & Planning (TCP). Licensure is state-by-state. Opens audit sign-off, public accounting, controller/CFO tracks.*
**Region:** United States (state boards; widely respected internationally) · **Typical cost:** Roughly $1,000–$1,500 in exam, application and registration fees for all four sections (state-dependent), before any review course.

**Who it's for:** Those targeting public accounting, audit, US tax, or corporate finance leadership who can meet the 150-hour education rule.

**Prerequisites:** Bachelor's degree + 150 total semester hours (specific accounting/business credits vary by state); 1+ year of supervised experience for licensure (not for sitting). This self-study path builds the knowledge base but does NOT satisfy the 150-hour or experience requirements.

**Maps to modules:** Financial Accounting Fundamentals, Intermediate / Advanced Financial Accounting, Auditing & Internal Controls, Taxation (US Federal), Managerial / Cost Accounting, Business Law & Ethics, Financial Statement Analysis

## CMA — Certified Management Accountant (IMA)  
*The Institute of Management Accountants' globally portable credential focused on corporate/management accounting and financial strategy. Two exam parts: Part 1 (Financial Planning, Performance & Analytics) and Part 2 (Strategic Financial Management). Emphasizes budgeting, cost management, internal controls, performance measurement, decision analysis, corporate finance and risk — i.e., the FP&A / controllership skill set rather than audit/tax. CMA vs CFA context: the CMA is the management-accounting/FP&A credential (internal decision support, costing, corporate performance), whereas the CFA (CFA Institute) targets investment management, equity/fixed-income valuation, and portfolio management for buy/sell-side finance. Choose CMA for industry finance/controllership; CFA for asset management, research, or investment banking.*
**Region:** Global (US-origin IMA; very strong in the Middle East, India, China) · **Typical cost:** Roughly $1,100 (students) to ~$1,600 (professionals) all-in for IMA membership, one-time entrance fee, and both exam parts.

**Who it's for:** Industry accountants and finance professionals on the FP&A, cost accounting, controller, or CFO track who want a global credential without the 150-hour CPA rule.

**Prerequisites:** Bachelor's degree (any field) from an accredited institution OR an approved professional certification, plus 2 years of continuous relevant experience (can be completed within 7 years after passing). IMA membership required.

**Maps to modules:** Managerial / Cost Accounting, Budgeting & Performance Management, Internal Controls, Corporate Finance, Financial Statement Analysis, Decision Analysis & Risk Management

## ACCA — Association of Chartered Certified Accountants  
*A globally recognized chartered accountancy qualification structured in three levels — Applied Knowledge, Applied Skills, and Strategic Professional — plus an Ethics & Professional Skills module and a Practical Experience Requirement (PER). Built around IFRS and international standards, making it especially relevant outside the US (and in IFRS jurisdictions like Saudi Arabia and the wider GCC). Confers the 'ACCA' designation; widely portable across employers and countries.*
**Region:** Global (UK-origin; dominant in UK, EU, Africa, GCC/Middle East, South & SE Asia) · **Typical cost:** Per-exam fees plus registration/annual subscription; full journey typically runs into the low-to-mid four figures (GBP) over several years, excluding tuition.

**Who it's for:** People wanting an internationally portable, IFRS-based chartered qualification — particularly outside the US, or in multinationals and IFRS-reporting markets.

**Prerequisites:** Minimum entry varies (school qualifications); degree holders and the FIA route earn exemptions. Requires 36 months of relevant experience (PER) and the Ethics module for membership. Registration + annual subscription.

**Maps to modules:** Financial Accounting Fundamentals, IFRS / Financial Reporting, Managerial / Cost Accounting, Auditing & Assurance, Taxation, Corporate Finance, Business Law & Ethics, Financial Statement Analysis

## EA — Enrolled Agent (US Tax)  
*The IRS's own credential and the highest designation the IRS awards — an EA has unlimited rights to represent taxpayers before the IRS. Earned by passing the three-part Special Enrollment Examination (SEE): Individuals; Businesses; and Representation, Practices & Procedures. No degree required, making it the most accessible US tax credential. Note: effective March 1, 2026 the SEE is administered by PSI Services (previously Prometric).*
**Region:** United States (federal tax practice) · **Typical cost:** $317 per part (three parts) at scheduling; plus the PTIN fee and a modest enrollment fee. No mandatory courses.

**Who it's for:** Tax preparers and anyone wanting federal tax-representation rights without a CPA license or accounting degree.

**Prerequisites:** No education or experience prerequisite — just a PTIN and passing all three SEE parts (or qualifying IRS work experience). Background/tax-compliance check applies.

**Maps to modules:** Taxation (US Federal — Individuals), Taxation (US Federal — Business Entities), Tax Practice, Representation & Ethics

## CIA — Certified Internal Auditor (IIA)  
*The only globally recognized designation for internal auditors, from the Institute of Internal Auditors. Three-part exam: Part 1 Essentials of Internal Auditing; Part 2 Practice of Internal Auditing; Part 3 Business Knowledge for Internal Auditing. Built on the Global Internal Audit Standards (formerly the IPPF). Signals competence in governance, risk management, control, and the audit process.*
**Region:** Global · **Typical cost:** Roughly $740–$1,000 for IIA members (application + three parts) up to ~$1,500 for non-members.

**Who it's for:** Internal auditors, risk and controls professionals, and accountants moving into governance/assurance roles.

**Prerequisites:** Bachelor's degree (or qualifying education/experience combination) + internal-audit experience (e.g., ~1–2 years depending on education), a character reference, and IIA standards adherence. Education and experience can be staged.

**Maps to modules:** Auditing & Internal Controls, Governance, Risk & Compliance, Financial Accounting Fundamentals, IT & Information Security Basics, Financial Management & Business Acumen

## CFE — Certified Fraud Examiner (ACFE)  
*The Association of Certified Fraud Examiners' specialist anti-fraud credential. The exam has three sections — Fraud Schemes & Financial Crimes (rebranded from Financial Transactions & Fraud Schemes), Fraud Investigations & Legal Issues (law), and Fraud Prevention & Deterrence — each requiring 75% to pass. Combines accounting, investigation, law, and criminology. Highly valued in forensic accounting, investigations, and compliance.*
**Region:** Global · **Typical cost:** CFE Exam application ~$480 (covers first attempt at each section) + ACFE membership dues; retakes ~$110 per section. Official prep materials (Fraud Examiners Manual/prep course) are paid-optional.

**Who it's for:** Auditors, accountants, investigators, and compliance professionals specializing in fraud detection, deterrence, and forensic accounting.

**Prerequisites:** Associate ACFE membership required. A points-based eligibility system (education + fraud-related experience): minimum 40 points to sit, 50 to be certified, plus at least 2 years of relevant professional experience for certification.

**Maps to modules:** Internal Controls & Fraud Prevention, Forensic & Investigative Accounting, Business / Commercial Law, Financial Statement Analysis, Auditing & Internal Controls

## CPA Canada (note — successor to legacy CGA/CMA/CA)  
*Context note: Canada unified its three legacy designations (CGA — Certified General Accountant, CMA-Canada, and CA) into a single 'CPA' designation around 2012–2015. The legacy CGA route no longer exists; today's path is the CPA Professional Education Program (PEP) plus the Common Final Examination (CFE) and 30 months of practical experience, administered provincially. Do not confuse Canada's CPA with the US CPA — they are separate bodies and credentials, though both are IFRS-aware (Canada uses IFRS for public companies, ASPE for private). This self-study curriculum maps to the prerequisite/foundational knowledge, not the proprietary PEP modules.*
**Region:** Canada (provincial CPA bodies; CPA Canada national) · **Typical cost:** Several thousand CAD across PEP modules, the CFE, and dues — set provincially. The proprietary materials are not free.

**Who it's for:** Anyone pursuing professional accounting in Canada, or learners who encounter the obsolete 'CGA' term and need the current pathway.

**Prerequisites:** Undergraduate degree with specific prerequisite coursework, completion of CPA PEP, passing the Common Final Examination (CFE), and 30 months of qualifying experience. (Prerequisites can be met via CPA preparatory courses.)

**Maps to modules:** Financial Accounting Fundamentals, IFRS / Financial Reporting, Managerial / Cost Accounting, Auditing & Assurance, Taxation (Canada), Corporate Finance

## IFRS Certificate (ACCA CertIFR / DipIFR; ICAEW IFRS Programme)  
*Standalone International Financial Reporting Standards credentials for professionals who need IFRS competence without a full chartered qualification. ACCA offers the Certificate in International Financial Reporting (CertIFR — introductory, online assessment) and the more advanced Diploma in IFR (DipIFR, for experienced practitioners). ICAEW offers an IFRS Learning & Assessment Programme. Especially relevant in IFRS-reporting regions (EU, GCC, Saudi Arabia). These are paid assessments, but the underlying standards and much study material are free via the IFRS Foundation.*
**Region:** Global (IFRS jurisdictions; not the US, which uses US GAAP) · **Typical cost:** ACCA CertIFR ~£190 (assessment only) to ~£295 (with materials); DipIFR exam fee similar. ICAEW IFRS programme is materially more expensive (around US$2,000). The certificate exams are not free.

**Who it's for:** Accountants and finance staff in IFRS-reporting countries (or multinationals) who need a focused, recognized IFRS credential.

**Prerequisites:** CertIFR: open entry, no prerequisites. DipIFR: typically 2–3 years of relevant accounting experience or a recognized qualification. ICAEW programme: open but aimed at practitioners.

**Maps to modules:** IFRS / Financial Reporting, Intermediate / Advanced Financial Accounting, Financial Statement Analysis, Consolidations & Group Accounts

## SOCPA — Saudi Organization for Chartered & Professional Accountants (Saudi note)  
*Saudi Arabia's professional accountancy body (under the Ministry of Commerce) and the national standard-setter. Key point for this curriculum: Saudi Arabia has adopted IFRS Accounting Standards as issued by the IASB for publicly accountable entities (effective 2017) and IFRS for SMEs (2018), with limited local additions — so IFRS-based study transfers directly. SOCPA runs the Fellowship examination (accounting, auditing, Zakat & tax, fiqh/jurisprudence, and business law) for the SOCPA designation, and also offers a standalone Professional Certificate in IFRS. The Saudi-specific layers to add on top of an IFRS foundation are Zakat & tax (ZATCA) and Saudi commercial/Sharia law.*
**Region:** Saudi Arabia (GCC relevance) · **Typical cost:** Set by SOCPA (exam and membership fees in SAR); the Fellowship and IFRS certificate exams are paid. Underlying IFRS study can be done with free resources.

**Who it's for:** Accountants practicing in Saudi Arabia/the GCC who need the local professional designation, and IFRS learners who want to localize for the Saudi market.

**Prerequisites:** Fellowship: relevant degree and experience plus passing SOCPA's multi-subject Fellowship exam (rules set by SOCPA). The IFRS professional certificate is more accessible. Exact requirements are governed by SOCPA regulations.

**Maps to modules:** IFRS / Financial Reporting, Auditing & Assurance, Zakat & Tax (Saudi/ZATCA), Business & Commercial Law, Financial Accounting Fundamentals
