# The Saudi Track: Mastering Accounting for the Kingdom (SOCPA Overlay) 🇸🇦
> **Learn accounting the world over — then make it count in the Kingdom. A free, open path from global fundamentals to fully SOCPA-, ZATCA- and Vision 2030-ready practice.**

[← Back to the main curriculum](./README.md) · [Browse as a website](./docs/)

This track is the Saudi Arabia (KSA) jurisdiction overlay for the Open Accounting Curriculum — a free, OSSU/Odin-Project-style path to becoming a working accountant. It does NOT replace the standards-agnostic global core; it layers on top of it. The global core teaches universal principles (the accounting equation, double-entry, the IFRS conceptual framework, audit logic, professional ethics). This overlay teaches what a Saudi accountant must do differently to apply those principles legally and competitively inside the Kingdom.

Why a separate track? Saudi Arabia does not adopt raw IFRS — it applies "IFRS as endorsed in KSA" through the Saudi Organization for Chartered and Professional Accountants (SOCPA), which runs a structured endorsement process (translation plus technical, Sharia and legal review) before any IASB standard becomes binding, sometimes adding local disclosures. Tax is unusual too: the Kingdom runs a dual system of Zakat (a 2.5% Islamic levy on the zakat base for Saudi/GCC-owned shares) and a 20% Corporate Income Tax (on non-Saudi/foreign-owned shares), both administered by the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA, formed in 2021 by merging GAZT with the General Authority of Customs). On top of that sit 15% VAT (since 1 July 2020), mandatory e-invoicing (the FATOORAH program, Phase 1 from December 2021 and Phase 2 integration in waves from January 2023), a new Companies Law (effective 19 January 2023), and a payroll regime built on GOSI social insurance, the Wage Protection System (WPS) and end-of-service benefits under the Labor Law.

All of this is accelerating under Vision 2030, which is professionalizing the finance function, deepening the Saudi Exchange (Tadawul) capital market, and driving demand for Saudis who hold the SOCPA Fellowship. This overlay is your map to that opportunity. Each Saudi module names the global core module(s) you should finish first, then tells you exactly what changes in the Saudi context.

## Key facts
- Currency: the Saudi Riyal (SAR), pegged at SAR 3.75 per USD.
- Standards framework: 'IFRS as endorsed in KSA' for public-interest entities and IFRS for SMEs for other entities, endorsed and supplemented by SOCPA (not raw IASB adoption).
- Professional body & standard-setter: SOCPA — the Saudi Organization for Chartered and Professional Accountants, which also confers the SOCPA Fellowship.
- VAT: standard rate 15%, in force since 1 July 2020 (raised from 5%), administered by ZATCA; zero-rated and exempt categories apply.
- Zakat vs. Corporate Income Tax: Zakat is 2.5% of the zakat base on Saudi/GCC-owned shares, while a 20% Corporate Income Tax applies to foreign-owned shares; withholding tax ranges 5%-20% — all under ZATCA (formed 2021 from GAZT + Customs).
- E-invoicing (FATOORAH): mandatory under ZATCA — Phase 1 (generation) from 4 December 2021 and Phase 2 (integration/clearance via the Fatoora platform) rolled out in waves from 1 January 2023.
- Companies Law: the new Saudi Companies Law took effect on 19 January 2023, providing a single framework for all entity types (with a bylaw-alignment compliance deadline that ran to January 2025).
- Payroll/social insurance: GOSI contributions plus the SANED unemployment scheme, the Wage Protection System (WPS) and statutory end-of-service benefits under the Saudi Labor Law govern employment costs.

### SA.2 — IFRS as Endorsed in KSA & the SOCPA Endorsement Framework

> 🇸🇦 **Read these global modules first:** 1.4, 1.5, 5.3

⏱️ **7**

SOCPA (the Saudi Organization for Chartered and Professional Accountants, renamed from the former "Saudi Organization for Certified Public Accountants") is the body that endorses accounting standards in KSA under the Ministry of Commerce. It requires publicly accountable entities (listed and other large entities) to apply "IFRS that are endorsed in the Kingdom" — full IFRS as issued by the IASB plus a small set of additional Saudi disclosures and pronouncements for matters not covered by IFRS — effective for periods beginning 1 January 2017, while non-publicly-accountable entities apply "IFRS for SMEs as endorsed in KSA" from 1 January 2018. This module explains the endorsement mechanism (translation plus technical, Sharia and legal review), the transition timeline, the few points where endorsed KSA practice diverges from pure IFRS, and how zakat — a 2.5% religious levy on a net-worth zakat base assessed by ZATCA — is presented in IFRS financial statements.

> **Why it matters:** Almost every set of audited financial statements you will prepare, audit, or analyze in Saudi Arabia is signed off as complying with "IFRS as endorsed in SOCPA," not plain IFRS — so a practitioner must know exactly what the endorsement adds and removes, or risk an incorrect compliance assertion. The SOCPA Fellowship exam tests the endorsement framework, the 2017/2018 transition dates, the publicly-accountable-entity scoping that drives full-IFRS-vs-IFRS-for-SMEs, and the distinctly Saudi treatment of zakat in the primary statements. Getting the zakat presentation and the SOCPA-specific disclosures right is a frequent differentiator between a generic IFRS preparer and someone genuinely competent in the KSA reporting environment.

**By the end you'll be able to:**

- Explain SOCPA's role and the endorsement mechanism (translation, then technical, Sharia and legal review with public consultation) and state precisely what 'IFRS that are endorsed in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia' means relative to IFRS as issued by the IASB.
- Apply the publicly-accountable-entity test to determine whether an entity reports under full endorsed IFRS or under IFRS for SMEs as endorsed in KSA, and cite the transition dates (listed/publicly accountable entities: periods beginning 1 January 2017; other entities: 1 January 2018).
- Identify at least three specific ways endorsed KSA practice can differ from pure IFRS — SOCPA-added disclosures for Sharia or local-law reasons, pronouncements covering matters not addressed by IFRS (notably zakat), and transition-period measurement constraints — and confirm these additions do not by themselves prevent an IFRS-compliance assertion.
- Describe how zakat is presented in KSA IFRS financial statements (historically within the income-tax/levy caption and the open IFRS 18 presentation debate) and distinguish a 2.5% zakat charge on a net-worth zakat base from income tax on accounting profit.
- Locate and cite the authoritative sources for a compliance conclusion: the SOCPA professional-standards pages, the IFRS Foundation jurisdiction profile, and ZATCA guidance on zakat.
- Draft a SOCPA-compliant basis-of-preparation and zakat disclosure note for a Saudi entity.

### SA.3 — Saudi Taxation I — Zakat, Corporate Income Tax & Withholding Tax (ZATCA)

> 🇸🇦 **Read these global modules first:** 4.1, 1.10

⏱️ **14**

This module is the Saudi replacement for the US-centric direct-tax content in global module 4.1. It covers the Saudi direct-tax system administered by ZATCA (the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority, formed in 2021 by merging GAZT and the General Customs Authority): zakat at 2.5% on the zakat base for the Saudi/GCC-owned share of an entity, corporate income tax at 20% of net adjusted profit on the foreign/non-GCC-owned share, and the mixed-ownership split that allocates a single company between the two regimes. It also covers withholding tax (5%–20%) on payments to non-residents, the arm's-length transfer-pricing rules, and the filing/assessment cycle. Learners read the global accounting-for-income-tax and conceptual-framework modules first, then layer this Saudi-specific overlay on top.

> **Why it matters:** Direct tax is the single most jurisdiction-specific topic an accountant faces in Saudi Arabia: there is no US-style income tax on individuals or fully Saudi-owned firms — instead a Sharia-based zakat and a separate income tax on foreign ownership coexist inside the same entity, a structure found almost nowhere else. Anyone preparing financial statements, computing a tax/zakat provision, or advising on inbound investment in KSA must split ownership, build a zakat base, gross up withholding on cross-border payments, and meet ZATCA filing deadlines. For the SOCPA fellowship exam, the zakat/CIT/WHT framework and ZATCA compliance are core examinable content and cannot be answered from US (4.1) knowledge alone.

**By the end you'll be able to:**

- Explain why a Saudi entity can be subject to zakat, corporate income tax, or both, and allocate taxable income/zakat base between the Saudi-GCC ownership share (zakat at 2.5%) and the foreign/non-GCC share (CIT at 20%) in a mixed-ownership company
- Compute a zakat base from a trial balance: start from equity/owners' capital, add long-term financing and provisions (sources of funds), deduct fixed assets, investments and deferred costs, then compare against adjusted net profit as the minimum base
- Calculate the 20% corporate income tax on net adjusted profit, identifying common book-to-tax adjustments (non-deductible provisions, disallowed expenses, depreciation differences)
- Apply withholding tax to payments to non-residents at the correct domestic rate (5% dividends/interest/rent, 15% royalties/payments to head office, 20% management fees, 5-20% technical/consulting services) and explain how a double-tax treaty can reduce the rate
- Describe the arm's-length principle and ZATCA transfer-pricing obligations (Controlled Transaction Disclosure Form, Local/Master file, Country-by-Country Report) and the relevant thresholds
- Map the ZATCA compliance cycle: registration, annual return filing within 120 days of year-end, WHT monthly remittance within 10 days, assessment, objection and appeal

### SA.4 — Saudi Taxation II — VAT & E-Invoicing (FATOORAH)

> 🇸🇦 **Read these global modules first:** 1.11, 4.1

> **Why it matters:** VAT and e-invoicing are the most frequent and unavoidable compliance touchpoints for any business operating in Saudi Arabia, and accurate VAT accounting and FATOORAH compliance are core expectations of any accountant, controller, or auditor working in the Kingdom. ### SA.6 — Saudi Payroll — GOSI, WPS, End-of-Service & Labor Law

> 🇸🇦 **Read these global modules first:** 1.11

⏱️ **8**

This overlay replaces the US FICA/FUTA payroll mechanics of global module 1.11 with the Saudi statutory payroll stack. Learners master GOSI social-insurance contributions on a basic-plus-housing wage capped at SAR 45,000. For Saudis who first contributed BEFORE 3 July 2024 (the "old system"), the long-standing combined rate is 21.5%, split employee 9.75% (9% annuities + 0.75% SANED unemployment) / employer 11.75% (9% annuities + 2% occupational hazard + 0.75% SANED). Under the 2024 Social Insurance Law reform, NEW labor-market entrants (no GOSI/pension contributions before 3 July 2024) follow a higher schedule whose annuities branch steps up 0.5% each July from July 2025 (9% → 9.5% in 2025, 10% in 2026, 10.5% in 2027, 11% by 2028): the combined rate is 22.5% for Jan–Jun 2026 (employee 10.25% / employer 12.25%) and 23.5% from 1 July 2026 (employee 10.75% / employer 12.75%). Non-Saudis carry only the flat 2% employer-only occupational-hazard contribution (SANED and annuities do not apply). Learners also master the Wage Protection System (WPS) enforced through the Mudad platform, and End-of-Service Benefit (EOSB) accrual and computation under Labor Law Articles 84–85. The Saudization/Nitaqat framework ties WPS and GOSI compliance to a firm's ability to hire and renew visas.

> **Why it matters:** Every private-sector employer in KSA must register with GOSI, pay wages through WPS/Mudad on time, and accrue EOSB — these are the bread-and-butter payroll obligations a Saudi accountant touches monthly, and getting the GOSI splits (including the date-sensitive old-vs-new-entrant schedule) or the EOSB resignation tiers wrong creates real liabilities and Nitaqat penalties. For the SOCPA exam and IFRS-as-adopted-in-KSA reporting, EOSB is a defined-benefit-style obligation under IAS 19 that must be measured and disclosed, while GOSI and WPS underpin the payroll cycle, internal controls, and compliance testing an auditor is expected to understand. US payroll mechanics (FICA, FUTA, W-2 withholding) simply do not apply, so a KSA learner needs this overlay to make 1.11 usable.

**By the end you'll be able to:**

### SA.7 — Auditing & Assurance in KSA (ISA as Endorsed)

> 🇸🇦 **Read these global modules first:** 2.4, 2.5

⏱️ **10**

External audit in Saudi Arabia is governed by SOCPA (the Saudi Organization for Chartered and Professional Accountants), which operates under Ministry of Commerce supervision per the Law of the Profession of Accounting and Auditing (Royal Decree M/59, dated 27/07/1442H, corresponding to 11 March 2021; Council of Ministers approval 9 March 2021). SOCPA endorses the IAASB's International Standards on Auditing (ISA), the International Standards on Quality Management (ISQM 1 & 2, ISA 220 Revised) and the IESBA International Code of Ethics for application to all mandatory audits in the Kingdom, with limited local additions (notably audit-documentation retention of at least 10 years under ISA 230 and required footnotes in the audit report). To sign a statutory audit report a practitioner must hold a SOCPA practising licence, and auditors of CMA-listed entities need additional CMA registration; firms are subject to SOCPA's mandatory quality-assurance review cycle (at least every three years for firms auditing public companies, banks and independent governmental organizations, and every five years for other firms). This module layers that Saudi institutional and statutory framework on top of the global auditing (2.4) and professional-ethics/independence (2.5) modules.

> **Why it matters:** Auditing is one of the six examinable SOCPA Fellowship subjects, and the exam tests the KSA-endorsed framework — not vanilla ISA — including the local additions and the statutory audit/exemption rules of the Companies Law (Royal Decree M/132, in force 19 January 2023). Anyone working in assurance, finance, or controllership in KSA must know which companies are legally required to be audited, who is licensed to sign the report, the exact framework reference an auditor cites, and how SOCPA quality review and the ethics/independence code constrain firms. This is foundational for working in or contracting with audit firms, listed companies, and ZATCA/CMA-regulated entities.

### SA.8 — Public-Sector & Government Accounting in KSA

> 🇸🇦 **Read these global modules first:** 5.1

⏱️ **9**

Saudi government accounting is undergoing one of the largest public-sector reforms in the region: a phased transition (the conversion program began in 2017 under Royal Decree No. 13059, issued 16 Rabi al-Awwal 1438H) from traditional cash-basis budgeting toward full accrual-basis accounting aligned with International Public Sector Accounting Standards (IPSAS), led by the Accrual Accounting Center at the Ministry of Finance (MoF). The SOCPA exam expects candidates to distinguish private-sector IFRS from public-sector IPSAS, to know that SOCPA technically supports the MoF reform, and to reason about transparency mandates. Practically, the private sector touches this every time it sells to government: invoicing, contract management, and getting paid all flow through Etimad, so the accountant who understands the public-sector ledger and the platform that feeds it is far more employable in KSA's public-finance ecosystem.

### SA.10 — Arabic-Language Resources for Saudi Accountants

> 🇸🇦 **Read these global modules first:** 0.1

⏱️ **12-18 hours**

A curated roundup of the best FREE Arabic-language resources for Saudi and Gulf accounting. It points learners to SOCPA's official Arabic IFRS and audit-standard translations, ZATCA's Arabic zakat/VAT/e-invoicing (FATOORAH) guides, and reputable free Arabic YouTube channels and MOOC platforms that teach accounting fundamentals and IFRS. This is an overlay module: a learner first reads the global English fundamentals in Module 0.1, then layers the correct local Arabic terminology (الزكاة، ضريبة القيمة المضافة، وعاء الزكاة) on top so they can read primary Saudi sources and work in an Arabic-first environment.

> **Why it matters:** Saudi accounting is practiced and regulated primarily in Arabic: the SOCPA Fellowship exam was historically Arabic-only, with an English option added only from the third 2025 sitting (September 2025); the legally authoritative versions of the VAT and zakat regulations are the Arabic texts (English ZATCA documents are explicitly "unofficial translations"), and day-to-day work — ledgers, tax returns, audit files, client communication — happens in Arabic. A learner who knows only English IFRS terms will struggle to read a ZATCA assessment, file an Arabic-language إقرار, or sit the SOCPA exam. Mastering the local Arabic terminology is essential for anyone planning to work in or qualify within the Kingdom.

**By the end you'll be able to:**

- Map 30+ core accounting, zakat and VAT terms between English and their official Arabic equivalents as used by SOCPA and ZATCA
- Navigate SOCPA's Arabic IFRS and audit-standard publications and locate the Arabic version of a specific standard
- Read and extract rules from a ZATCA Arabic guide (دليل) on VAT, zakat, or e-invoicing without relying on the unofficial English translation
- Follow Arabic-language YouTube and MOOC instruction on accounting fundamentals and IFRS, recognising spoken Arabic technical vocabulary
- Compute a VAT return at the current 15% standard rate and record zakat (2.5% of the zakat base) and VAT journal entries using Arabic account names
- Identify which Saudi source is legally authoritative (Arabic original) versus a convenience translation
